Job Application Checklist Before You Submit

A five-minute review can prevent avoidable mistakes such as applying to an expired role, uploading the wrong resume or missing a required field.

By HD Careers Editorial TeamUpdated 23 Oct 20266 min read

Before opening the form

  1. Confirm the role is still active on the employer or authorized portal.
  2. Re-check location, work mode, experience and qualification requirements.
  3. Note the requisition ID and deadline when available.
  4. Prepare the version of your resume that best reflects relevant, truthful evidence.

Before clicking Submit

  1. Contact details.
    Check phone number, email and spelling.
  2. Dates.
    Make sure education and employment dates match your resume and supporting records.
  3. Documents.
    Verify that you uploaded the intended file, not an older draft.
  4. Eligibility questions.
    Answer based on the employer's wording, not on what another job board says.
  5. Consent and declarations.
    Read them before agreeing.

Use a role-specific evidence check

Before applying, compare the employer's essential requirements with your actual experience. For each requirement, note one truthful example from coursework, an internship, a project or paid work. If a role asks for SQL, for example, a stronger resume bullet explains the dataset, the query you wrote and the result, rather than listing SQL without context.

Do not claim experience with a tool merely because it appears in the job description. Where the posting distinguishes required and preferred qualifications, prioritize the required ones first. If the employer does not specify a graduation year, do not assume that an unofficial social post's batch restriction is accurate.

Check the application destination

  1. Start with the exact job link and confirm the employer name, job title and requisition identifier match the listing.
  2. If redirected, check that the final destination is the employer's site or an identifiable authorized applicant-tracking provider. A generic careers homepage is not proof that a specific requisition is open.
  3. Do not pay registration, interview, security-deposit or equipment fees to obtain a job offer.
  4. When a role is closed or the requisition cannot be found, stop and search the employer's official careers portal rather than submitting personal information through a third-party copy.

Practical example: applying to a data internship

Suppose an internship lists Python, SQL and basic reporting. Your evidence might be a college project that imported a CSV, cleaned missing values using Python and summarized trends with SQL. Write what you actually did, include the project link if public and relevant, and keep the resume to the employer's requested format. If you have not used production data or cloud infrastructure, do not imply that you have.

For each application, save the exact URL, company, role, requisition ID, submission date and confirmation number in a private tracker. This helps distinguish genuine employer responses from unsolicited messages.

After submission

Save the application confirmation, requisition ID and any candidate-portal login details securely. Do not share passwords or OTPs with recruiters or third parties.
