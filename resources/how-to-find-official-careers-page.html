How to Find a Company's Official Careers Page

A recruitment platform domain does not automatically mean a job is fake. Many employers use third-party applicant tracking systems. The key question is whether the employer's own website leads you to that platform.

By HD Careers Editorial TeamUpdated 4 Nov 20266 min read

Start from the company, not from the forwarded link

Search for the company's main website and look for links such as Careers, Jobs, Join Us or Opportunities. This gives you an employer-controlled starting point. From there, the company may keep applications on its own domain or redirect you to an ATS.

Company-owned careers site vs ATS

Company-owned

Example pattern

careers.company.com
jobs.company.com
company.com/careers

Branding, domain ownership and the application flow are controlled directly by the employer.

Authorized ATS

Example pattern

company.wd3.myworkdayjobs.com
jobs.lever.co/company
boards.greenhouse.io/company

This can be legitimate when the employer's official careers site links to it. The ATS host itself should not be treated as proof of the employer identity.

Five checks

  1. Open the employer's official homepage independently.
  2. Use its Careers or Jobs navigation.
  3. Search the same role or requisition ID.
  4. Confirm the employer-owned page redirects to the same ATS/application URL.
  5. Re-check the final domain before uploading personal information.

Why HD Careers separates company logos from ATS favicons

An ATS favicon may show Workday, Greenhouse or Lever rather than the employer. HD Careers therefore treats the company identity and recruitment-platform identity separately. This prevents a generic ATS icon from being displayed as though it were the employer's logo.

Useful distinction: “third-party domain” does not mean “fake.” What matters is whether the employer authorizes that destination and whether the job details match.

Worked walkthrough: comparing a job-board link with the employer's site

Suppose a forwarded message points to a job with the title “Associate Software Engineer” and a requisition number. Instead of submitting a form immediately, open the employer's own homepage using a fresh browser tab. Find Careers, search the role title or requisition ID, and compare the location, experience and qualification requirements. If the employer's listing sends you to an ATS, note the exact destination hostname. Only then compare it with the forwarded URL.

Important distinction: the visible link text can say a legitimate company name while the destination points elsewhere. On a computer, inspect the actual link destination before clicking. On mobile, use the browser's link-preview or long-press option when available. Do not use the lock icon alone as a legitimacy test: HTTPS encrypts a connection, but it does not prove who runs the website.

When the job opens on Workday, Lever or Greenhouse

  1. Confirm the company Careers page links to that specific applicant-tracking provider, or find a corresponding job through the company-controlled search.
  2. Check the employer slug or tenant name. Similar-looking strings can refer to different organizations; do not guess.
  3. Match the requisition ID and job title. A generic “All jobs” page is not proof that one role is still accepting applications.
  4. Review eligibility, location and submission details again after any redirects; the destination is the final authority.
  5. If the listing is missing, withdrawn or replaced with a generic homepage, stop and report the outdated record to the source where you found it.

What if the company has no searchable careers page?

Some employers use a third-party recruiter or a form without a public searchable index. In that case, verify authorization using a company-owned contact address or an employer announcement, not the recruitment platform's logo alone. If you cannot corroborate the opportunity, keep it marked uncertain rather than describing it as definitely live or definitely fraudulent.

Common questions

Does a Google search result prove authorization? No. Search ranking and paid promotion do not independently verify a recruiter's identity. What if the role has no job ID? Compare company, full title, department, location, employer-controlled links and posting details. What if a listing uses a shortened link? Prefer the company Careers navigation and review the final URL before sharing personal information.
